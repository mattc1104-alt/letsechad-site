Test a Teaching

Deuteronomy 4:2 · Deuteronomy 13 · Deuteronomy 19:15 · Isaiah 8:20

Someone handed you a teaching. A video, a doctrine, a claim about what Scripture says. This tool walks you through the full test, one question at a time, and prints your worksheet when you are done. It works on anyone. On the loudest voice online. On your favorite teacher. On this ministry. And on you.

You weigh the teaching against the text, not against the teacher's charisma. A true thing from a flawed person is still true. A false thing from a magnetic person is still false. Pull the claim away from the personality and put it under the text, where charisma cannot help it. Your answers save in your browser as you go, so you can stop and come back.

Before You Begin

Name the claim

Write the teaching down in one or two plain sentences. If you cannot state it plainly, you cannot test it. Vague claims hide from the text.

The assistant opens the source, reads the context, checks the language, and hunts Tanakh for witnesses. It reads Tanakh references on Sefaria and Christian Bible references too, including the New Testament. When a New Testament verse quotes Tanakh, it lays the Tanakh text beside it. It gathers. It does not judge. It can err, so open every citation yourself before you lean on it. The gates below stay yours to answer. Clicking sends what you typed above to the research service so it can search. Nothing else leaves this page.

Phase One

Verify the claim

Most people skip this and lose before they start. Before you weigh whether a teaching is true, confirm it is even real. If it fails any one of these, you are done.

1. Go to the source. Does it exist, and does it say what they claim?

Open it yourself. This space runs on fabricated quotes, invented etymologies, a midrash no one can locate, a verse that is not there. Search Sefaria for a Tanakh reference, or BibleGateway for a Christian Bible reference. Watch the numbering: Jewish and Christian Bibles count some verses differently. The teaching often dies right here.

The claim failed verification. The teaching dies here. You do not owe a fabricated source a hearing. You can stop now, or keep going to see how the rest of the test works.

2. Read the context. Does the claim survive the surrounding passage?

Pull back to the verses before and after, then the whole passage. A verse can say one thing alone and the opposite inside its paragraph.

The verse does not say in its paragraph what the teacher says it says alone. The claim failed verification. You are done, unless you want to see the rest of the method.

3. Check the Hebrew. Does the translation carry the meaning?

Whole doctrines hang on one mistranslated word. Run the key word through a real tool like Pealim or Morfix, or compare translations on Sefaria. If the claim rests on a New Testament verse that quotes Tanakh, find the Tanakh text it quotes and check the Hebrew there. The quote may follow the Greek Septuagint where it differs from the Hebrew. Ask whether the teacher built a tower on a translation error.

A doctrine built on a mistranslated word is built on nothing. The claim failed verification.

Phase Two · The Measuring Line

The order of authority

  1. The Ten Commandments. Worship and the identity of G-d get settled here in one move. Israel heard these directly (Makkot 24a).
  2. Deuteronomy, with extra weight on Deuteronomy 13. Moshe's own explanation of the Torah (Deuteronomy 1:5) and the densest source of the commands and the tests themselves.
  3. The rest of Torah. The narrative, the case law, the rest of the five books.
  4. The Prophets. They apply, rebuke, and call Israel back. They never legislate new Torah and never cancel a command.
  5. The Writings. Inspired and authoritative, and the most open to interpretation. Read in light of everything above.
  6. Everything else. Talmud, midrash, the commentators. They illuminate and apply. They never override.

You are ranking clarity, not importance. The whole Torah binds. Stand on the clearest word first. A claim that fails there fails fastest, and you never have to defend a clear text with a murky one. Now run the claim through the gates below, starting at the top rung it touches.

Gate One · Deuteronomy 13

Which direction does it pull?

Toward the G-d of Israel and His Torah, or away from them. A sign, a feeling, or a fulfilled prediction does not save a teaching that moves you away (Deuteronomy 13). Direction overrides every wonder.

If it moves your worship, do not debate it. Leave. Deuteronomy 13 does not ask you to win the argument. It asks you to walk away.

Gate Two · Deuteronomy 4:2

Does it add or subtract a command?

Add nothing, subtract nothing (Deuteronomy 4:2). The obvious failures cancel a command. The sneaky ones add a new binding obligation that contradicts nothing, so it slips past a filter that only watches for what got removed. Watch both directions.

Flag it. A teaching that binds what Torah did not bind, or looses what Torah bound, fails at Deuteronomy 4:2 no matter how good it sounds.

Gate Three · Deuteronomy 19:15

Is there a second witness?

A matter is established by two or three witnesses (Deuteronomy 19:15). Apply this to the teaching, not to the text. A command G-d stated once stands on its own, because Torah said it. But a new doctrine built on a verse needs corroboration. Search the rest of Torah, the Prophets, and the Writings.

One anchor and no support is not established. It is one person's reading. You set it aside even when it contradicts nothing. That is not the same as calling it false.

The Teacher, Not the Teaching

How is it being sold?

The tests above weigh the teaching. These weigh the teacher. The enticer in Scripture works in secret and sells the new, the hidden, the thing they kept from you (Deuteronomy 13). Check any that apply.

None of these proves a teaching false on its own. Every one of them is a reason to slow down and run the full test before you move an inch.

The Verdict

Where the test points

Answer the questions above and the test will point somewhere. It has not pointed anywhere yet.

The test points. You decide. No tool renders a verdict for you, and neither does a teacher. These are four different actions, not a thumbs up or down. Choose the one you are prepared to act on.
